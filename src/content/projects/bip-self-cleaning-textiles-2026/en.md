htw saar's Blended Intensive Programme **Engineering Visions 2026** (Saarbrücken, Germany) brought together students from across Europe to work on ways to fight microplastics and PFAS: an online part plus two weeks on site. My team had six students from six institutions — mechatronics, chemistry, management, mechanical engineering, computer science and me, from systems engineering — and worked entirely in English.

## The problem

Synthetic textiles, polyester above all, are among the largest sources of microplastics: every wash releases microfibres that end up in rivers, the sea and the food chain. Most solutions act at the end of the pipe — filters, wastewater treatment — and treat the symptom, not the source.

## Our proposal

A vision paper that pulls together the evidence on self-cleaning clothing as prevention at source, along two routes:

- **Polyester with functional finishes** — photocatalytic (TiO₂), superhydrophobic and antimicrobial — so clothes stain, smell and shed less, and need fewer washes.
- **Biodegradable fibres** (Lyocell/Tencel) with bio-based coatings, so the fibres that inevitably come loose break down instead of piling up as plastic.

The conclusion: the decisive difference lies in the fibre, not the finish. And we were honest about the limit — the chain "self-cleaning → fewer washes → lower emissions" is a reasoned expectation, not yet a demonstrated result.

## How we worked

Brainwriting to generate ideas, grouping by theme, a mind map to narrow the scope and feedback from other participants until we reached the final question. Then a search for peer-reviewed papers and writing with twelve hands. At the end we presented the results orally, as a group.
